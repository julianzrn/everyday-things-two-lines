# Candidates — Everyday Things, Two Lines (Longer Run)

Competition: `u-suga-everyday-things-two-lines-longer-run-f8734479`
Brief: an everyday thing described in exactly two lines; the second line turns or complicates the first. Character counts include the newline between the two lines.

## Candidate 1 — SELECTED (377 characters)

A queue is a human train where every person is a carriage: it only moves when the front carriage moves, and nobody can unhook and rush ahead without breaking the whole train.
The train works because each of us, like a carriage, rolls forward only when the gap ahead opens — but it leaves out that carriages never get bored, check their phones, or quietly leave to buy a coffee.

Why it wins: the train metaphor is sustained across both lines instead of being dropped after the setup, and the second line both explains the mechanism and undercuts it with the human details carriages never have — boredom, phones, sneaking off for coffee. It is the only candidate whose two lines genuinely need each other.

## Candidate 2 (213 characters)

A grocery list is a letter to a future self who will stand in the aisle squinting, certain that "milk" was not the whole plan.
It never is: the list remembers every ingredient and forgets the hunger that wrote it.

## Candidate 3 (201 characters)

An elevator is a small room that teaches strangers the shared art of watching numbers change.
Everyone tracks the floors like a scoreboard, and nobody admits they pressed a button that was already lit.

## Candidate 4 (192 characters)

A password is a secret you repeat to a machine daily until the machine decides you no longer sound like yourself.
Then it asks you to prove who you are by naming a pet you never told it about.

## Candidate 5 (166 characters)

A fridge light is the only audience most leftovers will ever perform for.
It switches on faithfully at midnight, applauds nothing, and quietly bills you for the hope.

## Candidate 6 (165 characters)

A group chat is a meeting that never adjourns, held in a room everyone carries in a pocket.
The agenda is lost, the minutes are emojis, and someone is always typing.

## Candidate 7 (164 characters)

A house key is a tiny metal promise that the day will let you back in.
Lose it, and the most familiar door in your life turns into a stranger that needs convincing.

## Candidate 8 (149 characters)

A snooze button is a negotiation you hold with tomorrow, nine minutes at a time.
Both sides know exactly how it ends; the ritual is the entire point.
